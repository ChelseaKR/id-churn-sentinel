Privacy

What reading these pages and feeds sends, and to whom. Last changed 18 September 2026.

The short version

  • The feeds and data files are not tracked. feed.xml, changes.json, sources.json, status.json and every per-jurisdiction file are plain files. Fetching one runs no script and sends nothing to Google.
  • The two web pages count visits with Google Analytics 4: the front page and this one. Google Analytics does not load when your browser sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track, and you can switch it off in the footer.
  • There is no account, no sign-up and no email list. Nothing on these pages asks you to type anything.

What Google Analytics receives

When the front page or this page loads from chelseakr.github.io, a short script in the page loads Google's tag script, gtag.js, from Google's servers. Google Analytics then receives:

  • the page's address, cut down to the site and the path. Anything after a ? or a # is removed before it is sent;
  • the site you came from, cut down to its origin (for example https://www.example.org/), never the page or the search on it;
  • the page title, and whether you scrolled to the bottom of the page;
  • the address of a link to another website, such as a state agency's page or a PDF, when you follow it from these pages;
  • a random identifier kept in a cookie (see below), and your browser type, device type, screen size and language;
  • your IP address, which every server you connect to receives. Google uses it to estimate your approximate location (country, region and city) and states that Google Analytics 4 does not log or store it.

What it never receives

  • Anything you type. These pages have no form fields.
  • The query string or the fragment of the address.
  • Your name, your email address, or any identifier from this project. There are no user accounts, and the script sends no custom events.

Google signals and ad personalization are both turned off, and ad storage, ad user data and ad personalization are denied for every visitor. Google keeps event-level data for 14 months and then deletes it; aggregate counts in standard reports are kept longer.

Cookies

Google Analytics sets two first-party cookies on chelseakr.github.io: _ga and _ga_98S46JC943. They hold a random identifier and expire two years after your last visit. chelseakr.github.io also serves other projects by the same author, and the _ga cookie is shared with them, so the analytics on those sites receives the same identifier.

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, analytics storage is denied by default. There is no consent banner to change that, so no Google Analytics cookie is set there, and Google receives cookieless pings instead.

Turning it off

  • Send Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track from your browser. These pages then do not load Google Analytics at all.
  • Use “Opt out of analytics” in the footer. It stores your choice in this browser under id-churn-sentinel:analytics-opt-out, which applies to this site only. “Opt back in” removes it. The control needs JavaScript; without JavaScript, Google Analytics cannot run either.
  • Install Google's opt-out browser add-on: tools.google.com/dlpage/gaoptout.

Why a trans-ID project counts visits at all

This project's own risk audit says that a list of people reading about trans identity-document law is a targeting risk, and until 18 September 2026 these pages loaded nothing from any third party for that reason. On that date the project's owner decided to count page visits here, as on her other public sites. The decision record states the risk it accepts and the limits above. If that risk matters to you, send Global Privacy Control or opt out, or use the feeds, which carry none of it.

What GitHub sees

The pages and feeds are hosted on GitHub Pages, with raw.githubusercontent.com as a mirror. GitHub's access logs record the IP address of every request, including which per-jurisdiction feed was fetched. This project does not receive, control or delete those logs. To leave no trace, fetch the feeds over Tor or a VPN, or mirror them and serve them yourself (AGPL-3.0-or-later allows it).
